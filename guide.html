Section 1

Retrospective Chart Review

The most common first resident project — and the most likely to become disorganized if the protocol, data request, or database structure are vague. Follow each step in order; you cannot start data collection without IRB approval.

idea + mentor + protocol → HSRO / IBISResearch → approval → Data Broker → REDCap / abstraction → analysis → writing → submission
1

Define the question and build a protocol

Before contacting any office, align with your mentor on the exact study question, design, and scope. Write a short protocol (2–4 pages) defining the exposure, comparison group, and outcome. Keep the variable list lean.

Have this ready before any external contact
  • Working title and one-sentence research question
  • Study population: inclusion and exclusion criteria
  • Study period, primary outcome, and secondary outcomes
  • Rough variable list — lean is better, you can add later
  • Faculty mentor name and likely authorship team
2

Submit through HSRO / IRB in IBISResearch

All human-subjects research at UM — including retrospective chart reviews — must be submitted in IBISResearch for IRB review. The UM IRBs serve as IRB of record for Jackson Health System.

IBISResearch Submission
  • IRB portal: ibis-research.miami.edu/IRB
  • HSRO guidance: How to Submit to the IRB
  • Not sure if you need IRB? NHSR Self-Certification Tool
  • Review timelines: Exempt ~2 wks · Expedited ~2 wks · Full Board ~6 wks
💡 RECAP Tip: At UM, investigators are not permitted to self-determine whether their research qualifies for exemption. All Human Subjects Research — including studies that may qualify as exempt — must go through IBISResearch for a formal IRB determination. Data collection may not begin until that letter is issued.
3

Request the dataset through Data Broker Services

Once the study is cleared by IRB, request clinical data through Data Broker Services. This service handles UChart (Epic) extractions, large data pulls, and enterprise data transfers for research.

How to Submit a Data Broker Request
  • Portal: uhealth.service-now.com/esc — SSO + MFA login
  • Navigate to: Catalog Filters → Data Services → Data Broker Services (Clinical) Request
  • Under Service Requested: Select "Clinical Data (Research/Operations)"
  • Attach: IRB letter, protocol, and complete variable list with definitions
  • Track your request: uhealth.service-now.com/esc?id=my_requests
  • Pre-request scoping: use Epic SlicerDicer in UChart for exploratory cohort counts before committing
💡 RECAP Tip: Start focused. Residents often ask for too many variables early. A lean first request is faster to fulfill and easier to clean — you can submit a follow-up request if needed.
4

Build the REDCap database before abstraction

REDCap is the safest default for most resident chart-review projects — free to all UM faculty, staff, and students, HIPAA-compliant, hosted by UM IT, and exports directly to SPSS, Stata, R, or SAS.

Getting Started with REDCap
  • Create account at redcap.miami.edu — CaneID login, no separate access form required
  • Click "Request New Project" → complete the Decision Tree Survey → ServiceNow ticket auto-generates
  • Support: help@med.miami.edu with subject line "REDCap" — auto-routes to a REDCap administrator
  • Training: BCCC and Calder Library Biomedical Data Services both offer REDCap-specific sessions
💡 RECAP Tip: Build the REDCap project before abstracting any charts. If more than one person is abstracting, write a short abstraction manual and pilot on 5–10 records first. Messy abstraction creates messy analysis — and there is no fixing it after the fact.
5

Analyze the data

Choose the right route for your methods needs. If you are unsure which approach fits your question, consult BCCC before you start — it is free and saves time.

Path A

Self-Directed

Best when

Descriptive stats, basic comparisons, straightforward logistic or linear regression — and you are comfortable designing the analysis independently.

Free SPSS via UM IT it.miami.edu → SPSS software@miami.edu — software questions
Path B

BCCC Collaboration

Best when

Multivariable modeling, survival analysis, matching, sample size / power calculations, or any analysis you are not confident designing yourself.

Administrator MJRodriguez@biostat.med.miami.edu 305-243-4465 Fees Initial consult free · $105 / hr thereafter
Free Office Hours — 30 min, quick advice only

💡 RECAP Tip: Meet with BCCC before your final data pull, not after. Design issues caught early are fixable. Design issues discovered after abstraction often are not.
6

Write the manuscript

Do not wait for the final analysis to begin writing. Draft the methods section and table shells early — this often reveals gaps in your study design before it is too late to fix them.

📅
General Appointments
50-min sessions. Clarity, flow, organization, concision — available to all UM residents.
miami.mywconline.com
🔬
Research & Manuscript Consults
Grants, articles, abstracts, posters, and presentations — specialized consultations at Miller School.
medwritingcenterum.youcanbook.me
🏛️
CTSI Research Writing
Additional support for faculty, post-docs, and graduate students through the Miami CTSI.
miamictsi.org → Writing Support
💡 RECAP Tip: Go with a real draft and 2–3 specific questions — the Writing Center is most effective when you bring material, not a blank page. Some people draft methods → tables → results → introduction → discussion → abstract last. Others write the abstract first as a working outline of the whole paper. Either approach works; pick one and commit.
Section 2

Systematic Review or Meta-Analysis

Use this path when your study question will be answered by a structured literature review rather than patient-level data from your own charts.

1

Define the review type

Decide whether it is a systematic review, scoping review, or meta-analysis before searching. The type determines your protocol, databases, screening approach, and reporting standards.

Review TypeStandard & Key Distinction
Systematic ReviewPRISMA checklist. Focused clinical question; all steps pre-specified.
Scoping ReviewPRISMA-ScR + JBI Handbook. Maps breadth of evidence; broader inclusion.
Meta-AnalysisPRISMA + MECIR (Cochrane). Quantitative pooling — requires BCCC involvement.
Rapid ReviewAbbreviated methods; acceptable with explicit limitations stated.
2

Contact Calder Library early — most important step

Calder librarians collaborate directly on systematic reviews, scoping reviews, and meta-analyses for all Miller School, UHealth, and Jackson affiliates. All services are free.

✉ jxr1327@med.miami.edu — John Reynolds 🔗 medguides.library.miami.edu/evsynth
Collaboration Track

Full Co-Authorship

Complete multi-database search strategy, Covidence setup, methodological guidance, and drafting of the search methods section. Librarian becomes named co-author.

⏳ Waitlist — contact early
Consultation Track

Guided Self-Directed

Search strategy consultation, database selection, citation manager training, and attribution in the acknowledgments section.

✓ Shorter wait
💡 RECAP Tip: Systematic reviews typically take 12–18 months. Contact Calder Library as soon as you have a topic — there is typically a waitlist for the Collaboration Track.
3

Draft the protocol first

Write the PICO question, eligibility criteria, databases, outcomes, and screening plan before title-and-abstract review begins. Register in PROSPERO if you plan a systematic review — many journals now require it.

Before contacting Calder Library, complete
  • Cochrane Interactive Learning tutorials — free via UM institutional subscription
  • Review MECIR checklist (systematic reviews) or PRISMA / PRISMA-ScR (all review types)
  • Draft protocol with PICO question and inclusion/exclusion criteria
  • Identify target databases beyond PubMed: Embase, Cochrane, Scopus, etc.
4

Use Covidence for screening and extraction

Covidence is the standard tool at UM for dual-reviewer screening, full-text review, risk of bias assessment, and data extraction. It is free for all UM residents.

Covidence — Free Institutional License
  • Free unlimited license for all UM students, faculty, residents, and staff
  • UM info: support.covidence.org → Calder Library
  • Features: title/abstract screening, full-text review, risk of bias, data extraction, PRISMA flow diagram, multi-reviewer conflict resolution
5

Involve BCCC if planning meta-analysis

Ask BCCC for methods input before data extraction is finalized. Effect measure choice, heterogeneity assessment, and subgroup analyses must be pre-specified.

Section 3

Quality Improvement Project

Use this path when the primary goal is to improve a local process, workflow, or outcome in your clinic, service, or hospital setting.

1

Define the local problem and metric

Clarify the process you want to change, your baseline measure, your intervention, and your follow-up measure before involving anyone else.

2

Decide: QI only, or QI plus scholarship

If publication or dissemination is planned, contact HSRO early. Use the NHSR Self-Certification Tool if uncertain, or email HSROletterrequest@miami.edu for a written opinion.

3

Build a data-capture system

Use REDCap or another approved UM environment to track the measure consistently. See Section 1, Step 4 for full REDCap setup instructions.

4

Involve statistical support early

Before-and-after analyses, run charts, and statistical process control charts are significantly easier when planned before data collection.

✉ BCCC — MJRodriguez@biostat.med.miami.edu📞 305-243-4465
5

Keep the intervention simple and measurable

Define the measurement cycle before starting the intervention. QI projects work best when the intervention is clear and the outcome can be tracked over time.

6

Write with the discipline of a research report

Use SQUIRE 2.0 guidelines for QI reporting. Document the baseline problem, intervention, implementation period, metric, and limitations clearly. Writing Center support applies here as well.

Section 4

Submit and Publish

Before submission, confirm authorship, journal fit, figure requirements, and whether there are publication costs.

→

Check author instructions and AI acknowledgment policy

Review the journal's author instructions before final formatting, including current requirements for acknowledging AI use in writing or analysis.

→

Verify the journal is legitimate

Be cautious of unsolicited email invitations. Verify journals in DOAJ or check with a Calder librarian before submitting.

→

Check UM Open Access coverage before paying any APC

  • OA Contact: Jason Cohen — j.cohen4@miami.edu
